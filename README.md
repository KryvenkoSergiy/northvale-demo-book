# The Keeper's Log: a year at Northvale

Live: https://kryvenkosergiy.github.io/northvale-demo-book/

A portfolio demo by [Sergii Kryvenko](https://kryvenkosergiy.github.io/cv/): a site for a fictional novel. Ilse keeps a lodge on a lake island and logs a year of weather while waiting for Jonas to walk back across the ice. A prologue, 16 chapters (one per weather preset, four acts = four seasons) and credits, one full-screen stop each, rendered as real-time WebGL scenes.

React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind v4, three.js, React Three Fiber, postprocessing, GSAP, Web Audio. Deployed to GitHub Pages on every push to `main`.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev   # http://localhost:5173/northvale-demo-book/
```

`npm test` · `npm run lint` · `npm run build`

## How a stop plays
- [App.tsx](src/App.tsx): `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory` on `<html>` ([index.css](src/index.css)); an IntersectionObserver (0.6) picks the active stop, which plays its weather and moves the camera to its shot. Autoplay waits for the timelapse and for the whole entry to be on screen, then holds max(8 s, 4 s + 60 ms per character) with a countdown filling the next chapter in the timeline; the mouse over the subtitles pauses it, wheel, swipe or a key (other than Enter and ".") stops it.
- [content.ts](src/content.ts): acts, chapters (weather, date, shot, text), the book and its editions. `sentences()` splits an entry into subtitle sentences.
- [weather.ts](src/weather.ts): the 16 presets (sky, light, fog, `mist`, particles, foliage, snow, ice).
- [timelapse.ts](src/timelapse.ts): `lapse` holds `from`/`to` presets and 0–1 channels (light, sun, snow, ice, leaf, front, dusk, burst) that GSAP tweens; a season change runs a whole day with a night in the middle.
- [camera.ts](src/camera.ts): named shots (establishing, lodge, lake, forest, clouds, final) as orbits: azimuth, elevation, a target near the island's centre, zoom and fov. Every shot, and the intro's start, is a small variation of one front view (+z, the lodge's side) inside `FRONT`: azimuth within ±28°, elevation 12–32°, zoom 1–1.15 (above ~32° the island reads as a disc seen from above). Lake sits low and right, lodge close and left, forest high and left, clouds farthest and low with the target raised so the sky fills the top; the whole island stays in frame in every shot and every moment between them. `place()` computes the distance per frame from the screen: every `HULL` point (rings round the rim, the treetops and ridge, the rock cone's tip) must project inside the picture *between the letterbox bars* (`barHeight()` mirrors `BAR` in Sections.tsx; change both together). `mix()` moves along the orbit (angles, the short way round), so a move never cuts through the island. `rig` blends from wherever the camera is to the next shot; [camera.test.ts](src/camera.test.ts) projects the hull for every shot, every move at ¼, ½, ¾ and the intro, at 2.39:1, 16:9 and a 9:19.5 phone, and checks they all stay inside `FRONT`.
- Subtitles ([Sections.tsx](src/Sections.tsx)): the whole entry is laid out in the bottom bar from the start (hidden sentences keep their place, so nothing reflows); sentences come in one after another and stay, the earlier ones dimmed to 0.7 until the last arrives. A click, tap, Enter or "." shows it all at once; a stop already shown in full shows at once when you come back. The type is sized from the bar height and the width so the longest entry (178 characters) fits above the timeline from 375 px up.
- `lapse` and `rig` are plain mutable modules: GSAP writes them, `useFrame` reads them, React never re-renders for a frame.

## Scene
- [Scene.tsx](src/Scene.tsx): canvas, sky, lights, fog. [Island.tsx](src/Island.tsx): terrain, lake, instanced trees, rocks and grass, the lodge, the finale's lantern. [Range.tsx](src/Range.tsx): the valley far below, a ring of low-poly mountains rising out of a drifting sea of clouds (it replaces the dark haze below the horizon), lit by the same sun and snowed on with the island; it takes its own haze instead of the scene's fog, which would erase it in every foggy chapter. [ground.ts](src/ground.ts): the island's shape (`heightAt`, lake, lodge) and the shared snow material `snowy()`.
- [Residents.tsx](src/Residents.tsx): Ilse, Birk the dog, Aarne and his ferry, Jonas, loons, fox, stag, geese, fox tracks; low-poly figures built from primitives in code. A `Cameo` lists the chapters a figure appears in and where; it swaps halfway through a weather change.
- [WeatherEffects.tsx](src/WeatherEffects.tsx): rain, snow, petals, leaves, lightning, clouds, aurora, ground mist. [Post.tsx](src/Post.tsx): DOF, bloom, god rays, chromatic aberration, vignette, grain and a per-season LUT ([lut.ts](src/lut.ts)).
- Quality ([Scene.tsx](src/Scene.tsx)): four tiers, high with 4× MSAA (dpr ≤ 2), high without it, low (dpr ≤ 1.5, no DOF, god rays, chromatic aberration, MSAA or shadows, a third of the particles and grass) and low at dpr 1. [quality.ts](src/quality.ts) starts touch or under-768 px screens on low; after the first frames drei's `PerformanceMonitor` steps down a tier whenever most of 2.5 s runs under 45 fps, never back up. MSAA goes first: postprocessing multisamples every buffer of the chain, so it costs about a third of a dpr-2 frame. Dropping shadows recompiles the lit materials once.
- Portrait screens: `place()` widens the fov so a shot keeps at least the width a square screen would see; the fit then keeps the island inside the narrow picture.
- Reduced motion: `frameloop="demand"`; the page calls `invalidate()` on each cut. No intro, handheld, drift, timelapses, flashes, autoplay or walking figures; thunder runs on a timer, since no frame loop does.
- No WebGL (or the renderer failing to start): an error boundary around the Canvas swaps in a gradient in the act's colour. R3F's own `fallback` renders inside `<canvas>` either way, so it can't be used. The bars, subtitles, chapter nav and sound still work: they run off `lapse`, not the canvas.
- [audio.ts](src/audio.ts): Web Audio ambience layers per weather plus a music loop, the weather slightly muffled when the camera looks down from high above (`rig.height`); off until the sound toggle. Streamed, see Gotchas.

## Page UI
[Preloader.tsx](src/Preloader.tsx) holds a black card until the first compiled frame; [Intro.tsx](src/Intro.tsx) plays once per tab session (`sessionStorage`, skippable): title card, bars close in, the camera descends to the prologue. Letterbox bars carry everything, so nothing covers the frame: [Header.tsx](src/Header.tsx) (← CV, the book's mark, Start the story, Order the book, sound), the chapter kicker and subtitles ([Sections.tsx](src/Sections.tsx)), the chapter [Timeline.tsx](src/Timeline.tsx). [OrderDialog.tsx](src/OrderDialog.tsx) is a demo order form: native `<dialog>`, nothing is sent.

## Assets
- `public/models/*.glb`: Quaternius Stylized Nature MegaKit (CC0) and the lodge, compressed with `npx @gltf-transform/cli@4.5.1 optimize <in> <out>.glb --compress meshopt --texture-compress webp --texture-size 512 --simplify false --palette false --instance false` (no Draco: its decoder would come from a CDN). The lodge also needs `--prune-attributes false`: its materials have no texture in the file (they name one in `extras.texture`), so the UVs would be pruned. `audio/*.m4a`: HE-AAC (`afconvert -d aach`), built by `audio.py`.
- [scripts/](scripts/): `lodge.py` builds the lodge in Blender, `audio.py` turns the raw mp3s into the loops, `LICENSES.txt` lists every asset's source and licence. Raw sources are not in the repo; re-download them from `LICENSES.txt`.

## Gotchas
- Module-level assets outlive the renderer, and each renderer leaves dispose listeners on them (three's own shared DFG LUT too) that would keep it and its canvas alive after leaving the page. `Release` in [Scene.tsx](src/Scene.tsx) disposes everything in the scene on unmount and `Post` its LUTs; a new module-level asset outside the scene needs the same.
- `snowy()` sets `customProgramCacheKey`: its variants share one `onBeforeCompile` source, and three would otherwise reuse the wrong program.
- `snowy()` takes the rock texture (`rock`) and bare-of-snow (`snow`) as per-material uniforms, and smooth ones without a texture take `blank` as their map, so the cliffs and residents share one program and the lodge, its windows and the fox tracks another. A new snowy look should be a uniform, not a new variant.
- Lights stay in the scene and only dim: adding or removing one recompiles every material.
- Shared constants go in `.ts` files (`ground.ts`), not component files: the react-refresh lint rule forbids non-component exports there.
- Scene assets (geometries, materials) are built at module level: the React Compiler lint forbids mutating a `useMemo` value, and three objects get mutated.
- R3F adds every three element to the global JSX types, so a polymorphic `as: ElementType` prop no longer type-checks anywhere in the app; narrow it to a union of tags.
- Tone mapping runs as a post effect, so the Canvas is `flat` and `toneMapped: false` does nothing: HDR-bright materials (clouds, bolt, glow particles) are tuned against the post chain.
- One NaN in a shader turns black, and bloom spreads it over the whole frame. Clamp `pow()` bases (`max(x, 0.0)`) and don't damp angles towards exact values that end in subnormals.
- Props of `@react-three/postprocessing` effects stay constant: changing one rebuilds the effect. Animate the effect's uniforms instead (`Post` writes the DOF focus straight into its material).
- The repo has no Prettier config: don't run Prettier with defaults, it rewraps whole files.
- Frame rate: `Pace` in [Scene.tsx](src/Scene.tsx) renders at most ~60 fps (`frameloop="never"` + `advance`; reduced motion stays on demand). A dpr-2 frame takes ~10–13 ms of GPU on an M4 Pro, so on a 120 Hz ProMotion screen the uncapped loop ran at ~70–77 fps with every other frame 8 ms or 17 ms apart (judder), and the Claude preview pane froze for a whole second every few seconds. Measure pacing in a headed Chrome on the real screen: headless never presents, so it shows neither.
- `Ready` compiles into a render target (the scene never draws to the canvas; compiled for it, every program was built twice) and with hidden objects made visible for the call, then draws the whole scene once that way (frustum culling off) into the same 1×1 target. Compiling isn't enough: ANGLE on Metal finishes a program only on its first use, where three reads its info log, and that blocks ~0.5 s per program; without the draw, every chapter with new weather froze for 0.5–0.9 s mid-move. A first visit (cold shader cache) went from ~4.3 s to ~2.1 s to the first frame.
- Sound ([audio.ts](src/audio.ts)): all files are HE-AAC (1.7 MB in total, was 2.95 MB as plain AAC, which also lost the crickets' and blizzard's highs at low rates). HE-AAC's decoder fades in over ~30 ms, so a loop dips at every seam: each weather file carries 0.1 s of its own start after the loop, and `loop()` plays 0.05 s to 0.05 s + loop length (`PAD`/`SKIP`, kept in step with [audio.py](scripts/audio.py)). Weather loops are decoded only while heard and dropped after 3 s of silence; the score and thunder stream through `<audio>` (decoded, the score alone was ~200 MB of the tab), so the score keeps a ~20 ms gap at its phrase-break seam. Sound on adds ~30–60 MB to the tab, down from ~440 MB.
- Measuring startup in headless Chrome: keep vsync on, and inject a nonce into each shader's code (a comment is stripped) to defeat the OS Metal cache, or every run after the first is warm. With `--disable-gpu-vsync --disable-frame-rate-limit` the frame loop spins on the empty scene while the models load, and the first frame then waits ~2.5 s on that queue, which real browsers never see. Those flags are fine for FPS headroom.
- The card still on the CV's Work page (`public/work/northvale/preview.webp` in the CV repo) was taken in headless Chrome (real GPU): 1920×1080, reduced motion emulated, scroll to `[data-stop="8"]`, capture the centre 1920×803 band (the bars sit outside it), `cwebp -q 80 -resize 1200 0`.
- Reduced motion and no-WebGL: the app reads them on mount, so set them before it loads, e.g. DevTools → Rendering → `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, or headless Chrome with `--disable-webgl`.
